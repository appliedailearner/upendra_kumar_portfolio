🛑 Stop Guessing: The Microsoft Guide to Erasing Assumptions in Azure Migrate Business Cases
When you execute an initial Azure Migrate Business Case, the platform builds an instant financial model comparing your current local datacenter against a future state in Azure. But here is the major industry secret: Microsoft is guessing your on-premises costs.

Because the Azure Migrate assessment engine cannot natively read your company's ledger or private facility contracts, it injects highly researched, industry-standard benchmarks into the On-Premises Cost Section. While these models are directionally accurate, presenting un-customized averages to a CFO or financial procurement lead will invite immediate skepticism.

To construct an undeniable Proof of Execution (PoE) and secure corporate sign-off, you must replace generic assumptions with your actual local costs. This comprehensive guide breaks down the hidden mathematics behind Azure Migrate’s default engine, provides the precise discovery questions to ask your stakeholders, and highlights the official resources needed to automate your discovery safely.

🏎️ The Core Concept: The "Hardware Refresh" CapEx Cliff
Before digging into rows of a spreadsheet, your financial stakeholders must understand why remaining on-premises introduces passive structural risk.

On-premises infrastructures are governed by a rigid Hardware Refresh Cycle—typically a 3 to 5-year operational lifespan 🗓️. Once a physical server or storage frame crosses this window, three critical failures occur:

Support Escalation: Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) warranties expire, causing post-warranty maintenance support contracts to spike exponentially.
Operational Risk: Mechanical parts hit their failure curve, risking unexpected application downtime 🛑.
Performance Degradation: Older silicon cannot efficiently run modern microarchitectures or security containers.
Staying on-premises forces an enterprise into a Capital Expenditure (CapEx) Cliff—writing a massive upfront check to replace physical assets simply to maintain status quo performance. Migrating to Azure shifts this to an Operating Expenditure (OpEx) Model, transforming an unpredictable, spike-heavy capital layout into a predictable monthly operational utility cost.

[ Year 1-3: Hardware Stable ] ───► [ Year 4-5: Warranty Expires / Risk Spikes ] ───► 💥 [ CapEx Cliff: Massive Hardware Refresh Invoice ]
🔍 Behind the Scenes: How Azure Migrate Collects Data Safely
A common point of friction for IT infrastructure teams is whether running an assessment will impact production workloads or create security exposure.

To ensure high-fidelity discovery with zero business disruption, the data collection is completely non-invasive:

The Lightweight Appliance: Azure Migrate uses a single, agentless virtual machine deployed directly into the local hypervisor environment (VMware vSphere or Microsoft Hyper-V) 🖥️.
Read-Only Operations: The appliance operates with strictly read-only management privileges. It does not install software packages inside the customer's production operating systems 🛡️.
Continuous Performance Monitoring: Instead of taking a single point-in-time snapshot, the appliance polls the infrastructure continuously. It aggregates this metadata into 10-minute data points over a 1 to 2-week period, tracking real-time CPU cycles, memory allocation, disk throughput, and IOPS 📈.
📊 The Right-Sizing Payoff
This continuous stream of live data allows Azure Migrate to evaluate actual utilization rather than provisioned capacity. The assessment engine uses the performance history to recommend Right-Sized Azure resources, frequently proving that workloads can run safely on smaller, lower-cost cloud instances than their physical counterparts 📉.

🗂️ The Definitive Discovery Checklist & Azure Migrate Mapping
To override Microsoft’s benchmark numbers and deliver a bulletproof business case, deliver these targeted questions to your internal technical and finance teams.

💻 Pillar 1: Compute & Facilities Infrastructure (Target: Infrastructure & Facilities Leads)
Microsoft's default calculation assumes standard linear depreciation models for physical x86 servers and a colocation real estate model with a generic Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) of 1.80 (meaning 40% of facility power is wasted on cooling overhead).

Question 1: What is the average age of the physical servers hosting the target workloads, and when is their next major hardware refresh expected? 🔄
Maps to: Compute > Server Capital Cost (CapEx) & Depreciation Timeline
Question 2: Are these physical servers owned outright as Capital Expenditure (CapEx), or are they leased/operated under an active Colocation (OpEx) contract? 🏢
Maps to: Compute > Acquisition Model Type
Question 3: If utilizing a colocation facility, what is the remaining duration of the contract, and what is the annual cost per rack or square foot? 📜
Maps to: Facilities > Real Estate & Rack Rental Costs
Question 4: What is the estimated monthly utility cost (power and cooling/HVAC) specifically allocated to the data center or server rooms? ⚡
Maps to: Facilities > Power Consumption & PUE
💾 Pillar 2: Storage & Data Management (Target: Storage & Backup Administrators)
Azure Migrate assumes standard enterprise storage hardware acquisition costs a flat $2.00 per GB with an annual maintenance cost calculated at 10% of the raw hardware value.

Question 5: What is the total raw capacity (GB/TB) and the age of the current Storage Area Network (SAN) or Network Attached Storage (NAS) infrastructure? 📦
Maps to: Storage > Storage Capital Cost (CapEx)
Question 6: What is the annual maintenance and support contract cost paid to your storage vendors (e.g., Dell EMC, NetApp, Pure Storage)? 🛠️
Maps to: Storage > Storage Maintenance & Support
Question 7: What are the current hardware and licensing costs associated with your backup and disaster recovery infrastructure (e.g., backup targets, secondary sites, software licensing)? 🛡️
Maps to: Storage > Backup & DR Infrastructure Costs
🔑 Pillar 3: Software Licensing & Agreements (Target: Procurement & ITAM Teams)
By default, Azure Migrate assumes standard retail pay-as-you-go pricing for Windows Server and SQL Server unless you explicitly inject your existing corporate licensing agreements.

Question 8: Do you have an active Microsoft Enterprise Agreement (EA) or Software Assurance (SA) that would allow you to utilize the Azure Hybrid Benefit (AHUB)? 📜
Maps to: Licensing > OS & Database Licensing (AHUB Optimization)
Question 9: What are the current annual licensing and support costs for your virtualization layer (e.g., VMware vSphere/vCloud Foundation or Red Hat Enterprise Linux)? 🦊
Maps to: Licensing > Hypervisor / Virtualization Software Costs
👥 Pillar 4: IT Labor & Operations (Target: IT Director / HR Finance)
Microsoft assumes a baseline internal administrator salary of $100,000/year (adjusted by geography) and applies a fixed server-to-administrator operational density ratio.

Question 10: What is the estimated fully burdened cost (salary, benefits, overhead) of an IT administrator, and how many full-time employees (FTEs) are dedicated solely to physical infrastructure maintenance (racking, patching, hardware troubleshooting)? 👔
Maps to: Labor > IT Administrator Salary & Resource Density Ratio
Question 11: What is the average annual expenditure on third-party managed service providers (MSPs) or external consultants supporting the on-premises infrastructure? 🤝
Maps to: Labor > Outsourced Operational Expenses
🚀 The Financial Superchargers: Unlocking Maximum Cloud Savings
Once you populate the Azure Migrate tool with your actual data, you can apply Microsoft's two most powerful cloud optimization mechanisms to dramatically drive down the projected Azure cost.

🎫 1. Azure Hybrid Benefit (AHUB)
When an enterprise purchases software licenses through an Enterprise Agreement (EA) with active Software Assurance (SA), they have already bought the asset. AHUB allows them to reuse those licenses in Azure as a "pre-paid coupon." Azure strips away the software licensing fee, dropping the VM cost down to the base Linux compute rate, saving up to 40%.

Windows Server Datacenter Benefit: Includes unlimited dual-use rights. The customer can run the license on-premises and in Azure simultaneously with no time limits, making long-term, phased migrations completely cost-free from an OS perspective.
🎟️ 2. The SQL Server 1:4 Enterprise Multiplier
For highly virtualized database environments, SQL Server Enterprise Edition offers an incredible virtualization bonus when moving to the Azure SQL General Purpose tier.

The Multiplier: 1 core of SQL Server Enterprise owned on-premises transforms into 4 virtual cores (vCores) in Azure SQL.
The Financial Impact: If the target cloud design requires 40 vCores of database capacity, the customer only needs to bring 10 cores of their existing on-premises SQL Enterprise licenses to cover the entire footprint. The remaining 30 cores can be retired to eliminate future Software Assurance renewal costs.
[ 1 On-Premises SQL Enterprise Core ] ───► Unlocks ───► [ 4 Azure SQL vCores ]
