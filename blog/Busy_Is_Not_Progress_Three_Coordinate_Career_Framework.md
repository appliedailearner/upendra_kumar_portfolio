# Busy Is Not Progress  
## The Three-Coordinate Career Framework for Senior ICs and Managers

This idea is inspired by Brad Wiens: before an autonomous system moves, it answers three questions.

1) Where am I right now?  
2) Where am I trying to go?  
3) What’s the path between the two, and how do I correct drift?

Miss even one, and the system drifts.

Careers drift the same way. People work harder, stay busy, optimize tasks, and still stall. Not because they lack drive, but because their internal map is fuzzy.

This post turns “three coordinates” into a 90-day system you can run. It’s written for two tracks:
- Senior ICs (architects/engineers) leveling up to bigger scope
- Managers aiming for director

---

## TL;DR
You need three coordinates, written down:
- **Baseline:** one page of reality. Skills, proof, constraints, friction.  
- **Target Spec:** destination with constraints plus an ownership boundary.  
- **Execution Loop:** 90 days, weekly shipping, weekly correction.

Then you run one scoreboard every Friday so you don’t lie to yourself.

---

## The drift story most busy people are living
Career drift rarely feels like failure. It feels like “normal”:

- “I’m doing okay.”  
- “Once this project ends.”  
- “I just need more time.”  
- “Something will open up.”

Those aren’t plans. They’re placeholders. And placeholders quietly become permanent.

The cost is subtle:
- reliable but replaceable  
- skilled but under-positioned  
- busy but not advancing  

You don’t fix this with motivation. You fix it with navigation.

# Coordinate 1: Baseline  
## Current state, not your story

Most people plan from a story. Stories are comforting. Stories also protect self-deception.

A baseline is a one-page snapshot of reality. No spin. Just data.

**Rule:** If you cannot point to proof, you are running on reputation. Reputation decays. Proof compounds.

# Coordinate 2: Target Spec  
## Destination with constraints and an ownership boundary

Most goals fail because they are slogans:
- “I want growth.”
- “I want leadership.”
- “I want a better role.”

Those are not destinations.

A usable destination is a spec you can evaluate as true or false in 12–18 months.

### Target Spec (copy/paste)
```text

- Scope I want to own (ownership, not tasks):
- Domain focus:

- "No list" (what I refuse to do for 90 days):

Ownership boundary (critical)

- I INFLUENCE:
- I DO NOT OWN:

Proof required (pick 5)

2)
3)
4)

```

The “ownership boundary” is what makes this senior-level. It forces you to define where your accountability starts and ends.

## The bridge most people miss: JD to plan
Most candidates read a job description and think: “I need more skills.”

Senior hiring panels read a job description and think: “I need better proof.”

A job description is a list of outcomes the company expects you to drive. So your plan should reverse-engineer outcomes into proof.

---

# The JD-to-Plan Method  
## Turn any job description into a 12-week roadmap

This takes 15 minutes.

### Step 1: Extract 5 outcome pillars
Copy the JD into a doc. Highlight repeated verbs and nouns. Group them into 5 pillars.

Typical pillars at senior levels:
- enterprise delivery leadership  
- engineering excellence and governance  
- executive influence and decision-making  
- reusable assets and platforms  
- security, compliance, risk posture  
- innovation and thought leadership  
- presales and deal shaping  
- team leadership and talent growth  

Pick five. Not ten.

### Step 2: Convert each pillar into Proof
Every pillar becomes:

**Proof = Artifact + Metric + Story**

- Artifact: something you can show  
- Metric: a number that signals impact  
- Story: 2 minutes, Situation → Decision → Outcome  

Template:
```text
PILLAR → PROOF

Pillar:
- Artifact I can show:
- Metric that signals impact:
- 2-minute story (Situation → Decision → Outcome):
```

### Step 3: Convert proof into weekly shipping
Choose 3–5 pillars for 90 days. Then commit to shipping one deliverable every week tied to those pillars.

No deliverable. No progress.

# Coordinate 3: Execution Loop  
## A 90-day path with drift correction

A plan you write once is a wish list.

A path is a loop: ship, measure, correct, repeat.

### 90-day Execution Loop (copy/paste)
```text
Objective (90 days): one measurable sentence.

Key Results (3–5):
1) Ship X proof artifacts mapped to the target pillars
2) Create X executive narratives (1-pager or 5 slides)
3) Run X feedback cycles (peer/mentor/mock)
4) Build X reusable assets (templates/patterns/accelerators)

Weekly cadence (calendar it)
- Mon (30 min): choose one deliverable for the week
- Wed (15 min): unblock or cut scope by 50%
- Fri (30 min): score the week and commit next deliverable
- Month-end (60 min): reset KRs and delete low-ROI work
```

### The weekly deliverable rule (prevents overscoping)
Every weekly deliverable must have:
- a name  
- a reviewer  
- a definition of done in three bullets  

Template:
```text
Weekly deliverable:
Asset level (1/2/3):
Definition of done (3 bullets):
Reviewer:
Where it will live (link/location):
```

# The leverage move  
## Stop shipping effort. Start shipping assets.

Senior scope requires leverage. Leverage comes from assets others can reuse.

Rule:
**If you solve the same problem twice, convert it into an asset.**

### Asset Factory Menu
- Level 1 (1–2 hrs): checklist, decision matrix, one-pager  
- Level 2 (4–6 hrs): template pack, runbook, teardown post  
- Level 3 (8–12 hrs): case study, workshop, reference implementation  

Rule: ship one asset per week (Level 1 or 2). Ship one Level 3 asset per 90 days.

Assets make your value legible, and they compound.

# Proof Cards  
## Package your evidence like a product

Most people have experience. They just cannot present it.

Build 6–8 Proof Cards. These become your interview story bank and your portfolio.

# The Drift Score  
## A weekly scoreboard that prevents self-deception

Every Friday, score each item 0–2. Total /20.

1) Target spec is written and constrained  
2) Ownership boundary is written (own/influence/not own)  
3) I shipped a tangible deliverable this week  
4) Proof inventory is updated and easy to show  
5) External feedback happened in the last 14 days  
6) 90-day objective and measurable KRs exist  
7) Weekly review happened (calendar, not hope)  
8) “No list” is enforced  
9) I can explain the path in 60 seconds  
10) One recurring blocker was reduced  

### Drift triggers (non-negotiable)
- Miss 2 weekly deliverables in a row: cut scope by 50% and ship smaller next week.  
- No feedback in 14 days: schedule 2 reviews in the next 7 days.  
- Under 10/20 for two weeks: reduce to 3 pillars and rewrite target spec.

# Two paths, same system  
## Senior IC vs Manager aiming Director

The framework stays the same. The proof changes.

### If you are a Senior IC (architect/engineer)
Your proof must show:
- cross-team scope and architectural judgment  
- standards and governance that prevent drift  
- trade-offs under constraints  
- reusable assets adopted by others  
- security/compliance posture  

Your Proof Cards should emphasize decisions, integrity, and measurable outcomes.

### If you are a Manager aiming Director
Your proof must show:
- multi-team outcomes and operating cadence  
- decision-making under ambiguity  
- systems that scale people (standards, talent growth)  
- executive trade-offs (cost/risk/time-to-value)  
- recovery and stabilization ability  

Your Proof Cards should emphasize scope, influence, and repeatable operating models.
